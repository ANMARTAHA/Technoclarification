Overview

Just the rules, definitions and numbers that actually get tested — grouped by topic.

Clarification

  • Removal of suspended matter from a fluid medium.
  • Method depends on: particle size, physical state, quantity of suspended matter (slurry), fluid characteristics (viscosity, temperature, volatiles), speed needed.
  • Settling: liquid stands until matter settles or rises (density difference).
  • Separation by: skimming, decantation, siphoning.

Filtration

  • Removing solid particles by passing suspension through a porous/fibrous/granular medium.
  • Ultrafilters retain colloidal matter (bacteria, virus, pyrogen-retentive).
  • Filter medium must: give a clear filtrate at suitable rate, withstand mechanical stress, be chemically/physically inert.
Rate ∝ (area × pressure drop) / (viscosity × filter resistance)

Spirits

  • Alcoholic/hydroalcoholic solutions of volatile substances; alcohol usually >60%.
  • Lowest: Aromatic Ammonia Spirit (62–68%). Highest: Camphor Spirit (80–87%).
  • Prepared by: simple solution, maceration, distillation, chemical reaction.
  • Also called Essences. Store in light-resistant containers, cool place; equipment must be dry (oil precipitates with water → turbidity).

Elixirs

  • Clear, sweetened, hydroalcoholic oral solutions, flavored.
  • Classified: non-medicated (vehicle) vs medicinal (contains therapeutic agent).
  • Alcohol content: 4–40%. Self-preserving above 10–12%.
  • Adjunct solvents: glycerin, propylene glycol.

Extraction — major processes

  • Maceration: drug soaked in solvent (menstruum), agitated, filtered, brought to volume; leftover solid = marc. Best for drugs with little/no soft cellular tissue.
  • Percolation: drug packed in a percolator, solvent passed slowly through; more efficient than maceration.

Extraction — minor processes

  • Decoction: boil plant in water ~15 min, strain.
  • Digestion: maceration with continuous heat, 40–60°C.
  • Infusion: macerate in cold water, then add boiling water to 90% of desired volume.

Potency ladder of extracted products

  • Tincture: 10% (potent drugs) or 20–50% (less potent) activity.
  • Fluidextract: 1 ml = 1 g crude drug (100% activity).
  • Extract: 2–6× as potent as the starting material by weight.

Resins, oleoresins & glycerites

  • Resin: solid/semisolid plant or insect exudation, or produced by percolation with alcohol.
  • Oleoresin: mixture of volatile oil + resin (natural, e.g. turpentine; or synthetic, e.g. ginger oleoresin).
  • Glycerite: ≥50% glycerin by weight + medicinal agent — not a fluidextract.
